Element C

Design Requirements Problem Validation

Based on Filamentive, 33% of all 3D prints become waste, which generates approximately 379,000 kg of plastic waste. This can happen to anyone with a 3D printer, whether at home, at manufacturing, or school, which can cause severe environmental waste.

When determining the design requirements, we relied on our research and critiques from the product and patent research. Some products had specifications that were not user friendly or ones that did not function properly. Our group examined each product thoroughly to come up with the requirements we feel necessary to solve our problem statement.

Stakeholders

There are many stakeholders invested in trying to recycle and reuse their own filament. Our most common stakeholder will be individuals who own a 3d printer since they would be using this product the most. To add on, individuals who end up wasting many 3d prints would be highly invested in this product.

Build Requirements

The project requirements will be labeled as followed:

  • This build will shred: Most builds have these elements separate, which increases manual labor. When these two combine, all that’s needed is the 3D print to be placed in the shredded, and the build will do the rest.
  • This build will extrude: This build should be able to extrude the blended 3d printed filaments and spool it, thus recycling the 3d prints.
  • This build will be a desktop device: For the sake of simplicity, this device will be designed for desktop users in order for it to have a place next to 3D printers themselves. This will allow users to have devices at their desk at all times.
  • This build will be compatible for all spool sizes: Some 3D printers use different spool sizes, which can be hard for 3D printers that require smaller spools. Our build will ensure all spool sizes to create the one-size-fits-all ability, allowing users to make a less decisive decision for selecting our build.
  • This build must be extremely minimal in manual labor: Most remodelers require you to shred the material, then take that material to be placed into the remolder, which requires you to attempt manual labor before proceeding with the next step. When these machines are left unattended, the build will stall on manual labor until it’s complete.
  • This build will be one piece: Some builds require you to stretch and hook remolded filament to different parts for it to reach its final destination. When left unattended, the filament will extrude off track and will miss its target where it gets spooled.
  • This build will put safety first: This build may use thermal heat and moving parts containing sharp or large objects. Any segments like this must be kept out of reach to maximize safety.

    • To Sum it Up

    Our build will be designed to fit with those who use 3D printers for desktop uses, whether it’s at home, work, or other places. Many take some manual labor, which is a pain to endure just to minimize the amount of time the recycling process takes. With our build, the manual labor will be decreased and the machine will be completely automated.
