Forming the team and narrowing problems

After the personal pitch presentations, we began our project for PLTW Design and Development at Barrington High School. We divided into teams, and our group is Arjun, Kushal, and Varun.

We built a decision matrix to decide which problem we could realistically solve within one school year. After a project proposal, we narrowed our options to three problems:

Cable management Tennis ball machines 3D printing waste

We turned our research into summaries. Each summary lists the source, a description, why the source is important, and why it is credible.

With research underway, we expect to begin the next steps soon.

The decision matrix is on the Element A page.

Feedback from Mr. Ytzen

Today, after doing more research and refining our ideas, we met with Mr. Ytzen to review our remaining project concepts. He gave us the following feedback:

  • Tennis ball machine and 3D printing waste: Both are real problems, but they may be too complex to solve fully as currently scoped. They need more refinement.
  • Cable management: Not engaging enough to pursue, so we dropped it.

After the meeting, we focused on narrowing the two remaining concepts so each one addresses a single, manageable part of the problem. Arjun proposed one way to do this for 3D printing waste: grind failed or discarded prints into material that could be reformed and reused. We have not evaluated this idea yet. The period ended before we could discuss it, so we will pick it up at our next meeting.

Narrowing down to one problem

We decided to focus on recycling 3D printing waste, since it was the problem we were most interested in pursuing. Of our three options, cable management was considered the least engaging, especially by Mr. Ytzen, while tennis ball launchers and 3D printing waste recycling both proved difficult to build.

We also updated the layout and color scheme of our Canva notebook by brightening the grid lines. This makes the notebook easier to read and more visually appealing.
